# Arabic vs English (vs Multi-language) for a New Kids Cartoon YouTube Channel (Egypt/MENA creator, 2025-2026)

Research date: November 2026. Overall caveat: there is **no official YouTube data on kids-content RPM by country or by language**. Every country RPM figure below is a third-party estimate. Sources disagree, often by 2-5x, so treat the numbers as directional.

## Market size and competition: Arabic-speaking kids audience vs English; saturation

### Takeaway
English is the larger and richer market, but the top of it is dominated by giant studio franchises (CoComelon at about 200M subscribers, Little Angel, Pinkfong). Arabic is smaller, with about 380M speakers, and has fewer creators per viewer. It still has large incumbents: Toyor Al Janah has about 32-33M subscribers, and BabyBus Arabic and the Arabic dubs of global brands are also strong. I found no rigorous 2025-2026 measurement of a specific gap in Arabic kids content. The widely quoted "Arabic is 3% of internet content" figure dates from 2013-2015.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech counts about 380M Arabic speakers and about 1.9M Arabic creators. It lists Arabic with Hindi and Portuguese as "high-volume scaling" markets. English has about 119 viewers per creator, against about 2,480 for Hindi. AIR gives no Arabic viewers-per-creator ratio, but 380M/1.9M works out to about 200. — [AIR: languages to translate kids content](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-translate-content-for-kids-channels)
- The main CoComelon channel has about 200M subscribers in recent snapshots, against 166M in older ones. It was the 3rd channel worldwide to reach 100M, on Dec 12, 2020. — [Search summary of CoComelon sources](https://wiki.krisyotam.com/content/wikipedia_en_all_nopic_2026-03/CoComelon)
- Little Angel claims more than 150M YouTube subscribers and 1.4B monthly views across 25 languages. These are company-sourced figures from a 2026 interview. — [Señal News (Moonbug interview)](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/moonbugs-linear-channels-an-empire-that-transforms-family-routines/amp)
- Baby Shark Dance (Pinkfong) was the most-watched video worldwide for a 5th year running: 16.42B views and an estimated US$30M in ad revenue. Five of the top 10 most-viewed videos were kids' songs, with an estimated $84M combined. These are calculator-based estimates (BonusInsider via InfluencerMarketingHub), published Nov 21, 2025. — [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/why-toddlers-are-now-youtubes-real-ad-money-makers/)
- Arabic incumbents (third-party trackers, snapshots from different dates):
  - Toyor Al Janah main channel: about 32.7M subscribers (April 2025). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCozAuGJZ5MPoijz8IE_1Gtw)
  - BabyBus Arabic: about 10.5M subscribers on Social Blade's top-100 list for Saudi Arabia (list date not shown). — [Social Blade SA top 100](https://socialblade.com/youtube/lists/top/100/subscribers/all/SA)
  - Arabic Fairy Tales: 5.14M (June 2025). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCBqfiWktGq5Gy-B5fIaUdzw)
  - Tunes Kids: 2.28M (May 2025). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCN4McYJjT6KtaRDarrTqy3Q)
  - Kids tv Arabic: 1.65M (Aug 2025). — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC9ya8rlHpqsS3yIAqs55PeA)
  - Moonbug Kids Arabic also exists; no total subscriber count was found. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@moonbugkidsarabic/)
- Global brands also reach MENA in Arabic through licensing rather than their own YouTube channels:
  - Spacetoon is the master licensee for CoComelon in MENA, with Arabic dubs of CoComelon, Little Baby Bum and others. — [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/spacetoon-partners-with-moonbug-entertainment-to-bring-preschool-sensation-cocomelon-and-five-other-audience-favorites-to-mena/)
  - OSN launched a Moonbug Kids linear channel (Dec 2021) with Arabic dubs on its app. — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/1983121)
- The "Arabic is about 3% of internet content" figure comes from a 2013 Google statement and is repeated through about 2015-2018. Estimates range from under 1% to 3%. Lamsa's CEO says the gap is shrinking. — [Al Arabiya 2013](https://english.alarabiya.net/amp/media/digital/2013/12/01/Google-Arabic-content-ranks-eighth-on-the-internet); [MEED](https://www.meed.com/reaching-out-to-arab-users/); [Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/why-arabic-content-creation-is-a-major-business-opportunity-for-content-creators/)
- A Kidscreen piece reports underserved demand for kids content in some dialects, for example Lebanese, and growth in Arab kids content. I could not fetch the full text. — [Kidscreen](https://kidscreen.com/?p=204544)
- YouTube reach (YouTube/Google marketing data):
  - About 20M people in Saudi Arabia and 7.5M adults (18+) in the UAE (May 2024).
  - More than 60% of watch time for Egyptian content comes from viewers outside Egypt.
  - Channels earning seven figures a year grew 60% in Egypt and 40% in Saudi Arabia year over year (as of Dec 2024). — [Arab News: YouTube 20 years MENA](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2598185)
- Google launched an Arabic YouTube Kids app across MENA to grow locally relevant content. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/business/technology/arabic-youtube-kids-app-launched-in-mena-1.1197327)

### Inferences
- In English, a new channel competes directly with studio franchises that have 150-200M subscribers and huge back catalogs. Arabic has large incumbents too (Toyor Al Janah, BabyBus Arabic, dubbed global IP), but there are fewer of them and fewer creators per viewer.
- The fact that Egyptian content gets more than 60% of its watch time from abroad suggests Arabic content made in Egypt naturally travels to the Gulf and the diaspora, which pay more.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable figure for the size of the Arabic-speaking kids (0-12) audience on YouTube, or for its watch time against the English kids audience.
- I found no current (2024-2026) study that measures supply against demand for Arabic kids content.
- I found no subscriber counts for standalone CoComelon Arabic or Pinkfong Arabic YouTube channels.

## RPM/CPM for kids content: US/UK/CA/AU vs Gulf vs Egypt

### Takeaway
Kids content is the lowest-paying niche everywhere. AIR's 2025-26 median RPM for Kids & Teens is $0.33, because made-for-kids videos cannot show personalized ads. Inside that low base, US, UK, Canada and Australia views still pay several times more than Egyptian views, and Gulf views sit in between. A new Arabic channel's income therefore depends heavily on how much of its audience comes from the Gulf rather than Egypt.

### Cited Findings
- **Kids & Teens median RPM: $0.33** (P25-P75 range $0.16-$0.88), the lowest of 13 niches. It is attributed to made-for-kids videos having no personalized ads. For comparison, Entertainment is $2.43 and Education is $10.22. Sample: 300 channels, 3,595 channel-months, May 2025-May 2026, taken from YouTube Studio data. The study covers all countries and does not split kids RPM by country. — [AIR Monetization Statistics 2026](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
- Advertiser CPM by country, from AIR's June 2025 study of 3,000+ channels, all niches: US $14.67, Australia $13.30, Switzerland $12.98, India $0.74. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
- vidIQ estimates kids channels typically get CPMs of $1-4, against $8-20+ for comparable adult content. — [vidIQ CPM blog (via search)](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-cpm/)
- Middle East RPM, all content, from a guide updated Mar 2026 with no stated methodology:
  - Qatar $5-12; UAE $4-10 (body) or $4-8 (title); Saudi Arabia $3-8 or $3-6; Kuwait $3-8; Egypt $1-2 or $0.5-2.
  - The same guide says kids content lowers CPM by about 50%, gives no source for this, and claims a 500K-subscriber Egyptian channel earns about the same as a 100K Gulf channel.
  - The page contradicts itself internally, so its reliability is low. — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-middle-east-rpm-2026)
- Conflicting estimates for Saudi Arabia (all content):
  - Dynamoi creator RPM is about $0.69 (rank 46 of 87), falling from $1.76 (Nov 2025) to $0.45 (May 2026).
  - Another source gives a Saudi CPM of $2-6 and RPM of $1-2.50.
  - An Arabic calculator gives a Saudi CPM of about $1-3 against a US CPM of $5-15. — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/sv/data/youtube-adsense-rpm/sa); [search summary of CPM sources](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2025/08/youtube-cpm-rates-in-2025-how-location.html); [hijri-calendars estimator](https://hijri-calendars.com/youtube-earnings-estimator.php)
- Egypt estimates: CPM $0.50-2 and RPM $0.20-0.80 (Creaticalc), and about $0.69 on AIR's AdSense list. — [Creaticalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/youtube-cpm-rates-by-country); [AIR](https://air.io/en/monetization/how-much-can-creators-make-with-adsense-in-2025)
- Implied lifetime revenue for Baby Shark is about $30M / 16.42B views, roughly $1.8 per 1,000 views. This is derived from an estimate and is not actual payout data. — [Mediaweek](https://www.mediaweek.com.au/why-toddlers-are-now-youtubes-real-ad-money-makers/)
- US withholding tax for non-US creators:
  - The rate is 0-30% on earnings from US viewers, depending on the tax treaty. Creators must submit a tax form (W-8BEN) to claim treaty benefits. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10391362?hl=en)
  - With no form on file, withholding defaults to 24%. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/non-u-s-creators-24-percent-tax-youtube-revenue/)
  - Egypt's treaty rate for copyright royalties is reportedly 15%. This comes from a single secondary source citing IRS Table 1; verify it. — [search summary of guides](https://wiki.private.law/en/platform-withholding.md)
- Omar & Hana (a Malaysian Islamic kids cartoon) reported in 2021 that its YouTube monetisation was hit by US kids-data legal cases (COPPA-related) and the new US tax policy. — [Salaam Gateway, Jul 2021](https://salaamgateway.com/story/us-privacy-tax-laws-hit-malaysia-made-islamic-childrens-cartoon-youtube-revenues)

### Inferences
- These ratios are rough and rest on non-kids data. A US/UK/CA/AU kids view plausibly earns about 3-10x an Egyptian kids view, and a Gulf kids view about 2-5x an Egyptian one.
- For an Egyptian creator, about 15% US withholding (if the treaty rate is confirmed) reduces the US premium somewhat but does not remove it.
- Applying the AIR kids median ($0.33) at scale: 1M views/month is roughly $330/month, before any country mix effects. Kids channels need very high volume. This is why the winners (Toyor Al Janah, ChuChu, Moonbug) also pursue licensing, TV, streaming and merchandise.

### Gaps
- I found no source that gives kids-content RPM split by country (US vs Saudi vs Egypt). Kids-specific Gulf figures are entirely missing.
- I found no data on RPM for Arabic-language videos watched by viewers in the US or Europe (the diaspora).

## Discoverability: how hard is it for a new English vs Arabic kids channel to grow in 2025-2026?

### Takeaway
No hard comparative data exists. The qualitative evidence suggests that new kids channels everywhere depend on recommendations and the homepage, because kids rarely search. The English kids space has more professional supply per viewer, so a new English channel likely faces a steeper climb. Shorts and YouTube Kids eligibility are the levers agencies cite.

### Cited Findings
- Kids tend to watch whatever appears on the homepage rather than searching, so discoverability is the main barrier even for good shows. — [Kids Media Book Club (Substack)](https://kidsmediabookclub.substack.com/p/why-kids-media-is-more-complicated)
- Viewers per creator: English about 119, Hindi about 2,480 ("massive reach, low competition"), Arabic about 200 by calculation (380M / 1.9M). — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-translate-content-for-kids-channels)
- AIR (a growth agency, self-reported) says one kids channel saw +52% views and +33% revenue in two weeks after adding Shorts. Another AIR case reports growth in kids channels through Shorts and getting onto YouTube Kids. — [AIR case](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/1m-views-with-shorts-getting-on-youtube-kids); [AIR case 2](https://air.io/en/creators-spotlight/336-revenue-with-less-long-form-content-kids-channel-case-study)
- A kids animation studio argues the problem in 2026 is "low-quality repetition," not saturation. This is a vendor pitch. — [Whizzy Studios](https://www.whizzystudios.com/post/should-i-start-a-3d-kids-youtube-channel-in-2026-or-is-it-too-late)
- YouTube's policy on low-quality made-for-kids content allows suspension from the YouTube Partner Program, per a secondary summary. This matters for "mass-produced" AI kids channels in either language. — [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/?p=70508)
- In Saudi Arabia, 7 of the 10 most-watched videos were made by local Saudi creators, per a YouTube release. This points to a strong local-content preference. — [search summary, Arab News / Communicate Online](https://communicateonline.me/news/new-research-shows-popularity-and-reach-of-youtube-in-uae-and-saudi-arabia/)

### Inferences
- Arabic most likely gives a new channel a better chance of early traction: less professional competition per viewer and a preference for local content. English most likely gives a higher income ceiling per view but much harder early discovery. Both are inferences, not measured facts.

### Gaps
- I found no cohort data comparing how fast new English and new Arabic kids channels grow.
- I found no data on the reach of YouTube Kids (the app) in MENA compared with the US.

## Multi-language strategies: audio tracks, AI dubbing, separate channels, case studies

### Takeaway
Since September 2025, every creator can upload dubbed audio tracks to a single video. In YouTube's pilot, creators got over 25% of their watch time from non-primary languages. However, the kids franchises that won at multilingual scale (Little Angel, ChuChu TV, Kids Diana, Omar & Hana) mostly used **separate localized channels with human or professional dubbing**. In AIR's kids test, AI dubs held viewers far less well than human dubs. YouTube auto-dubbing supports Arabic, but without expressive speech, and lip sync is still experimental.

### Cited Findings
- **YouTube multi-language audio:**
  - Expanded to all creators on Sep 10, 2025, after a two-year pilot.
  - Pilot creators saw more than 25% of watch time from non-primary-language views (data as of Jul 2025).
  - Jamie Oliver got 3x views. Nick DiGiovanni expanded into Arabic-speaking markets, among others.
  - Multi-language thumbnails are in pilot. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/)
- Revenue from all language tracks is pooled on the one video. YouTube says different market CPMs do not hurt overall earnings (per a ppc.land report). — [ppc.land](https://ppc.land/youtube-makes-multi-language-audio-available-to-millions-of-creators/)
- **YouTube auto-dubbing:**
  - Arabic is a target language for English-original videos. An Arabic-original video can only be dubbed into English.
  - Expressive speech covers English to French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Portuguese and Spanish only, not Arabic.
  - Not eligible: music-only or low-speech videos, among others. This matters for nursery rhyme or song channels.
  - Lip sync is experimental and limited to select channels. — [YouTube Help: automatic dubbing](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972?hl=en); [YouTube Blog: expressive speech](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-auto-dubbing-expressive-speech/)
- **AI vs human dubbing for kids:** In AIR's test on a kids channel, professionally dubbed tracks averaged 5-6 minutes of view duration, against 54 seconds for the AI dub. AIR recommends AI to test markets and human or hybrid dubbing to scale. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-translate-content-for-kids-channels)
- **Separate-channel case studies** (AIR, agency-reported):
  - Kids Diana Show (138M subscribers) is translated into 20 languages; its Hindi channel has 29.1M subscribers and 13B+ views.
  - Vania Mania Kids' Spanish channel has 1.48M subscribers and 13M+ monthly views; its German channel ranked #7 in Germany for Kids Entertainment & Animation.
  - HZH's Indonesian dub ranked #6 in Indonesia, with 6.8B+ views. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-translate-content-for-kids-channels)
- **Omar & Hana (Digital Durian, Malaysia), the closest analog for an Arabic+English Islamic or values-based kids cartoon:**
  - Language order: Malay, then English, then Indonesian, then Arabic (Arabic launched Jul 2020 and passed 100K subscribers in under 6 months).
  - Later snapshots: Arabic about 1.5-1.8M, English "Islamic Cartoons for Kids" about 3.61M (Jul 2025), Malay about 7.76M.
  - The English channel had about 830K subscribers in 2021, with about 100K daily views (about 2M per day in Ramadan). — [The Vibes](https://thevibes.com/articles/culture/18448/arabic-omar-hana-youtube-channel-surpasses-100000-subscribers); [vidIQ Arabic](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCr3XqrfFHRy25Dcrm7RVD0w); [Social Blade Arabic](https://socialblade.com/youtube/handle/omarandhanaarabic); [vidIQ English](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC178EmfQAV3OT-UpuO6WUMg); [Salaam Gateway](https://salaamgateway.com/story/us-privacy-tax-laws-hit-malaysia-made-islamic-childrens-cartoon-youtube-revenues)
- **ChuChu TV** runs 12 language channels (English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, Spanish, Portuguese, French, and others) and expanded into Netflix licensing. **Moonbug** localizes with real children's voices and says deeper localization takes "more time and investment." — [Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/content/chuchu-tv-expands-global-footprint-with-netflix-deal/amp); [Señal News 2023](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/nicolas-eglau-we-want-to-have-more-touchpoints-with-our-brands)
- **Little Angel:** 25 languages, more than 150M subscribers (company claim). — [Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/interviews/moonbugs-linear-channels-an-empire-that-transforms-family-routines/amp)

### Inferences
- Pattern among winners: they build a home-market base first, then add language channels once the IP is proven. The second language does not need to be English first; Omar & Hana went Malay, then English, then Arabic.
- For songs and nursery rhymes, YouTube auto-dubbing won't work (music-only content is ineligible). The audio tracks feature also fits poorly with song-heavy content, because lyrics need re-recording. Expect separate re-sung channels or human-dubbed tracks.
- Audio tracks keep all views on one video (pooling signals and revenue). Separate channels allow localized titles, thumbnails, rankings and YouTube Kids placement in each market. Many kids networks chose separate channels. Note that this choice mostly predates the Sep 2025 general rollout of audio tracks.

### Gaps
- I found no published results for kids channels using multi-language *audio tracks*, as opposed to separate channels, since the Sep 2025 rollout.
- I found no current confirmation of whether audio tracks appear and work inside the YouTube Kids app.
- I found no subscriber figures for Arabic versions of Pinkfong, ChuChu TV or Little Angel.

## Can an Egyptian/Arab creator credibly produce English kids content?

### Takeaway
There is no direct evidence either way. Animation hides the creator's identity, so credibility mostly depends on voice quality (native-sounding English voices) and on the content. The strongest proof that "non-native studios can win in English kids content" comes from India (ChuChu TV, Chennai) and Malaysia (Omar & Hana English), not from Egypt. Egyptian creators have a clear advantage in Arabic, because Egyptian dialect is in high demand for dubbing.

### Cited Findings
- ChuChu TV, an Indian studio, has 60B+ views with an English-led network and a Netflix deal. — [What's on Netflix](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/kids-youtube-channel-chuchutv-with-over-60-billion-views-making-jump-over-to-netflix/)
- Omar & Hana's English channel, from a Malaysian studio, reached about 3.6M subscribers by mid-2025, a niche (Islamic values) English audience. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UC178EmfQAV3OT-UpuO6WUMg)
- UAE studio Blink Studios aims at international hits, but its show Karim and Noor launched on Spacetoon and YouTube in an Arabic-first context. TwelveP, a UAE animation studio, expanded on YouTube through digital distribution deals. — [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/arts-culture/television/uae-production-studio-blink-studios-has-sights-set-on-developing-an-international-hit-1.650921); [Drop Media](https://www.dropmedia.co.uk/uae-based-animation-studio-twelvep-expands-youtube-footprint-and-secures-slew-of-digital-first-distribution-deals/)
- In Arabic dubbing, "Egyptian is the most wanted" dialect (Hyphen, Oct 2025). — [Hyphen](https://hyphenonline.com/2025/10/07/voice-actor-dubbing-arabic-egyptian-bluey-captain-majid/)
- Egyptian voice actors who offer English and Arabic are available on freelance marketplaces (Voices.com and others). — [Voices.com example](https://www.voices.com/profile/nader-nabil)

### Inferences
- An Egyptian creator can make credible English kids animation by hiring native-accent English voice talent and by avoiding culture-specific cues that seem "off" to US parents. The easiest English niche to win is likely values or Islamic family content, where Muslim parents worldwide are the target. Omar & Hana shows this works.
- Competing head-on with CoComelon-style generic nursery rhymes in English, from Egypt, with no budget or IP, is the hardest scenario.

### Gaps
- I found no survey of parental trust in kids channels by creator nationality or accent.
- I found no documented case of an Egyptian studio running a large English-language kids YouTube channel.

## Recommendation: start in Arabic, English, or both, and when to expand

### Takeaway
On balance the evidence favors **starting Arabic-first**, while designing the content to be easy to dub from day one. Then **add English (and possibly other languages) once a format is proven**. Arabic offers easier traction and a native advantage. English offers higher revenue per view but stiff, well-funded competition. This is a synthesized judgment; the inputs are uncertain.

### Cited Findings
- Kids RPM is very low everywhere (AIR median $0.33), so volume and IP matter more than which country's ads a view gets. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-monetization-statistics-2026)
- Gulf RPM is far higher than Egypt's (Qatar $5-12, UAE $4-10 and Saudi $3-8, against Egypt $0.5-2, all content, low-reliability source). Other sources put Saudi much lower (about $0.69). — [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-middle-east-rpm-2026); [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/sv/data/youtube-adsense-rpm/sa)
- Multi-language audio tracks are available to all creators and gave pilot creators more than 25% of watch time from other languages. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/multi-language-audio/)
- In AIR's kids test, AI dubs held about 54 seconds of viewing against 5-6 minutes for human dubs. — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/top-languages-to-translate-content-for-kids-channels)

### Inferences
- **Phase 1 (months 0-6 to 12), Arabic.**
  - Choose between Modern Standard Arabic (fusha) and Egyptian or white dialect with the Gulf in mind. Egyptian dialect has broad appeal (it is the "most wanted" dubbing dialect). Standard Arabic is a safe choice for an educational positioning and for parents across the region.
  - Use mostly dialogue and story rather than pure songs if you want auto-dub or audio-track options later. Keep text out of the artwork, and keep characters culturally neutral where possible.
  - Target Gulf parents with topics, release times and Ramadan or Eid content to raise RPM.
- **Phase 2 (once a format reaches roughly 100K+ views per video, or after YPP approval), add English.**
  - Start by testing English audio tracks (cheap, AI or hybrid dubbing) on the best-performing videos.
  - If the English share becomes meaningful, launch a separate English channel with native-accent human voices, localized titles and localized thumbnails.
  - Omar & Hana and Kids Diana followed this pattern: separate channels once the IP was proven.
- **Both from day one** is only advisable with budget for professional English voices. It doubles production overhead and splits a new creator's focus.
- **English-first** makes sense only with a differentiated niche that has weak competition (for example, Muslim and values-based family content, or Middle Eastern culture told in English), plus native English voice talent.
- Always file the W-8BEN tax form to claim the treaty rate on US earnings.

### Gaps
- Without kids-specific country RPM data, nobody can reliably predict the income difference between Arabic-first and English-first. The creator should run the experiment: publish Arabic and add English audio tracks early, then compare RPM and retention by language in YouTube Studio.
